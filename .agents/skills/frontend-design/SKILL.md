# Frontend Design

Approach this as the design lead at a design studio known for giving every client a distinct visual identity that is not mistaken for anyone else's. This client has already rejected proposals that felt cliché or templated, and is paying for a distinctive point of view: make deliberate, opinionated choices about palette, typography, and layout that are specific to this brief, and take aesthetic risk if justified.

## Ground your designs in the subject matter

If the brief does not identify what the product or subject matter is, identify it yourself before designing, and confirm with the client. You can come up with one concrete subject, the design's audience, and the design's primary job, as a proposal. If there's any information in your memory about the client's preferences or context about what they're building, use that as a hint. The subject's industry, subject matter, materials, and vernacular are where distinctive visual choices come from. Build with the brief's real content and subject matter throughout.

## Design principles

1. **Hero & First Impressions**: For web designs, the hero is the first thing viewers will see. Open with the most characteristic thing in the subject's world, in the form that is most appropriate: headline, image, dynamic spotlight, or live demo. Avoid cookie-cutter layouts.
2. **Distinctive Typography**: Typography carries the personality of the page. Use intentional font pairings (e.g. geometric display font like Space Grotesk / Outfit / Syne alongside a pristine modern body sans like Inter / Plus Jakarta Sans). Avoid generic monospace body text or unreadable sci-fi fonts for standard paragraph reading.
3. **Color Palette & Atmospheric Depth**: Avoid raw primary colors or abrasive pure-neon clashes. Use deep dark canvas (obsidian, titanium, slate, subtle atmospheric ambient lighting) with purposeful high-contrast electric accents (emerald jade, neon coral, cyan aura) and refined translucent glass layers with subtle borders.
4. **Information Architecture & Visual Structure**: Structural devices like outlines, borders, badges, dividers, and tabs encode useful information rather than merely decorating.
5. **Micro-interactions & Tactile Feedback**: Smooth hover elevations, glowing ring indicators on active filters, responsive stock availability badges, intuitive cart drawer with instant feedback, and clean dialog transitions.
6. **Avoid AI Clichés**:
   - Avoid generic warm cream #F4F1EA or raw #000000 + 100% lime green with unstyled monospace.
   - Avoid standard cookie-cutter cards with equal padding and zero depth.
   - Use crisp, tailored microcopy, sharp badges, dynamic status indicators, and deliberate contrast hierarchies.
